Assessment

Sprint RSE 26Q3 S4 closed at 96.6% of items and 95.6% of story points — the best reported result in this series, ahead of the RSE 26Q2 S1 4/8-4/22 baseline by 7.6 and 4.6 points. Carryover of 4.4% of SP is half the baseline’s 9%. Zero story-point re-estimates across fifteen days, and all 21 true mid-sprint injections finished. Nineteen of twenty-one assignees closed everything assigned to them. Those are real numbers and the team should get credit for them.

The single biggest risk is that the sprint’s headline number was assembled, in part, in its last minute. 70.0 SP — 34.4% of total scope — was marked Done on the final day, against a sprint-long best of 29.0 SP across three days. Of that, 18.0 SP (25.7%) is traceable to close-out mechanics rather than observable delivery: 7.0 SP transitioned in the 35 seconds before the sprint-close action, 6.0 SP of items renamed and closed in the same interval (including TRI-54779, “Deploy to vehicle” → “Athena 2 Linux Box Validation & Testing”), and 5.0 SP of tickets created and closed the same afternoon. The adjusted push of ~52 SP is still the sprint’s largest day by far — but a sprint whose reported completion moves 3.5 points in its final 53 seconds is a sprint whose completion metric is partly an artifact of when someone clicked “close.”

The second risk is quieter and structurally worse: 6.0 SP of unfinished work left the sprint sequence entirely. Three of the four items unfinished at close (TRI-55284, TRI-55285, TRI-55360) are In Progress, unresolved, and absent from sprint 7644. Only TRI-55312 carried forward. So the flattering 4.4% carryover rate is flattering in part because two thirds of the carryover was dropped instead of planned. Combined with the 13 items descoped mid-sprint, whole-sprint completion against everything ever in scope is 86.8%, not 96.6% — while the dashboard’s churn rate reports 0.0% and sees exactly one resolved issue for the month.

Contamination tagging finished at threshold (80.8% of additions, 95.2% of true injections) and the nudge mechanism proved itself: TRI-55838 was nudged on 2026-08-26 and carried a Dependency Unblock tag by close. The remaining gap is the day-1 exemption — four untagged day-1 items, including the orphaned 3-SP TRI-55360 — and the runbook currently forbids nudging them. That exemption should be revisited. Read across the sprint the tag mix is reassuring: 16 of 30 tags are Dependency Unblock or Unexpected Capacity, work that became possible or that fit, and only 5 are Mis-Planning.

Four retro asks, in priority order
  1. Require a destination when an item leaves an active sprint, including at close. Six story points of In-Progress work vanished from the board on 2026-08-26 and no metric registered it. Largest measurement gap in the program; fired in four consecutive sprints.
  2. Stop treating “closed at sprint close” as delivery evidence. Record a work-complete date distinct from the Jira transition, or at minimum report final-day completion separately. A sprint that gains 3.5 points in 35 seconds is telling you about process, not velocity.
  3. Extend contamination tagging to the day-1 planning dump. Worst-performing bucket for four straight sprints (62.5% of SP here) and the only bucket the nudge cannot reach.
  4. Check in on Sem Onyalo and Vinay Puthalath before sprint 7644 gets going. Three items, 6.0 SP, zero completion — and their work is the work that got orphaned. That is capacity or dependencies, not effort.
The positive to carry forward without qualificationZero re-estimation churn across fifteen days, 100% estimation coverage, nothing sized above 3 SP, and every injected item finished. Whatever else is true, this team’s estimates hold and its unplanned work gets done.
