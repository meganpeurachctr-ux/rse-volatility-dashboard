Delivered SP exceeded pre-sprint SP in 11 of 12 sprints (median ~119%), and scope at close exceeded pre-sprint scope in all 12 (median ~125%). A fifth to a third of every sprint’s points did not exist at planning time; the completion percentage looks healthy because the denominator grows to match whatever got done.

Hedges: pre-sprint SP is a proxy and misreads 26Q2 S2 (16 SP pre-sprint) and 26Q2 S4 (70 SP day-1 dump). The honest denominator was only computed from 26Q3 S4 onward, where it costs 9–11 points — so earlier sprints are likely flattered by an unknown amount.

Assessment

S6 finished well on the reported number and middling on the honest one. 93.4% of SP against a roster that shed 16 items in its final week; 82.8% against everything ever in it; 80.4% net of churn. That last figure is comparable to S5’s honest 81.5% — so S6 is flat, not improved.

The measurement problem is now bigger than the delivery problem. 28.6% of delivered SP was recorded on the final day, 32 SP in the last 70 minutes, 9 SP in 33 seconds. Same-day status hygiene is the highest-leverage change available.

Eight items will vanish unless someone moves them — 17 SP, zero carried into S7. Four are Vinay Puthalath’s, all In Review: a review-capacity problem, not an effort problem.

Unplanned work won every contest with planned work. All 32 injections finished (100%) while pre-sprint work finished at 94.5% and all 16 descoped items were planned. The issue is not interrupt volume — it is that interrupts are never what gets dropped.

Tagging closed as an open failure: 75.0%, eight backfills unanswered after two nudges, all one assignee. Make the field required at creation or give the nudge an escalation path.
