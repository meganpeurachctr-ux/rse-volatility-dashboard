The finding that matters most: about a third of the sprint was recorded complete on the last day

At the 2026-08-11 (Day 14) reading, 68 items were done. Final is 100. ~32 items / ~64 SP — roughly a third of scope and SP — were recorded complete on close day, 2026-08-12. Intermediate readings drifted by up to 2 items, so treat the delta as ±2.

Two explanations, opposite conclusions:

Status-tracking lag. Work finished progressively but tickets were not transitioned until close day. Delivery was healthy; the daily burndown was wrong all sprint — which makes every mid-sprint carryover projection in this series unreliable and explains why 2026-08-10 and 2026-08-11 both over-predicted carryover by roughly 2×.

Genuine last-day crunch. A third of the sprint really landed in the final 24 hours. The 95.2% is real but the delivery profile is heavily back-loaded, carrying quality risk on work with no review runway.

The evidence leans to status-tracking lag. Several close-day closures carry completion write-ups describing multi-day work — TRI-54837's RGBD training runs took ~3.6 h and ~8.6 h and were launched 2026-08-09; TRI-54838's RGB runs launched 2026-08-07. The work predates the close-day transition by days. This is the single most useful thing to take to retro: the board was not a reliable picture of progress during the sprint.

Data Integrity — read before trusting the daily series

Three reading-drift incidents occurred on 2026-08-12, all under-reporting completion:

  1. Revision 1 (written 10:14 PDT) reported 86/106 done. Four items had already resolved at 07:50, 08:26, 09:09 and 09:49 PDT. Its Jira reads were taken near the start of the run, not at write time.
  2. Revision 2 (10:30 PDT) reported 90/106 done. Eight further items resolved between 10:39 and 13:12.
  3. Intermediate readings under-counted John Shepherd's queue by 2 items / 3 SP — read as 11 items when the closed-sprint state shows 13.

Reconciliation, verified: 191.5 SP (10:30) − 5 SP moved to S4 + 3 SP reading correction + 2 SP re-estimates = 191.5 SP final. Scope 108 actual − 3 moved = 105 items.

Process changes before the next sprint:

  1. Stamp every snapshot with "data as of HH:MM" separate from file write time. All three incidents would have been visible immediately.
  2. Re-read Jira immediately before writing the file, not once at the start of the run.
  3. Add a summary-diff check between consecutive snapshots. The five in-place rescopes were invisible to every existing metric.
  4. Add an estimate-change check. The two 2→3 SP changes were caught only by comparing SP distributions between readings.
  5. Treat mid-sprint carryover projections as unreliable until the status-lag question is answered. 2026-08-10 and 2026-08-11 both over-predicted by roughly 2×.

Assessment

RSE 26Q3 S3 7/29-8/12 closed on 2026-08-12 at 100/105 items (95.2%) and 183/191.5 SP (95.6%), beating both the RSE 26Q2 S1 baseline (89% / 91%) and the prior sprint RSE 26Q3 S2 (93.8% items). Injection was well controlled at 13.3% of items and 10.7% of SP, roughly two-thirds of both comparators, with no single-day spike and full contamination-source coverage on true injections for the first time in the series. Total unfinished work came in at 8 items / 13.5 SP (7.0% of SP), below the baseline's 9% carryover. On the numbers, this is the strongest sprint in the tracked series.

Three things temper that. First, about a third of the scope was recorded complete on close day, and the evidence — multi-day training runs closed hours before the sprint ended — points to status-tracking lag rather than a genuine final-day surge. The board was not a reliable picture of progress, which is why every mid-sprint carryover projection in this series was roughly 2× too pessimistic. Second, five items were retitled in place on close day with no change to created date or story points, so no volatility metric registered them; one of those, TRI-54187, was narrowed from implementing a stop button to investigating cancellation and then closed as Done, which inflates completion against what was committed. Third, two items were re-estimated upward from 2 to 3 SP minutes before being closed, adding 2 SP to recorded velocity.

Carla Bellido's queue is the one genuine delivery concern. She finished at 50% (2 of 4), both completions landing in the final 45 minutes; her two unfinished items are the largest single block of remaining work. She was flagged in four consecutive snapshots as the only assignee at zero, and the pattern held to the end. Krish Sharma also carries 3 unfinished items across the sprint boundary (one on the closed sprint, two moved to S4), all Harness PoC or blocked-on-IT work.

One housekeeping item: the five items that remained on the closed sprint rather than rolling into S4 should be checked — if unintentional they will not appear on the S4 board.
