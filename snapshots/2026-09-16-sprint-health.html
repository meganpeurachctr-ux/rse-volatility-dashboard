Assessment

The 2026-09-15 verdict was wrong, and the reason is a clock artifact worth fixing. The 2026-09-15 snapshot judged S6 on a 10:13 PDT read and concluded the improvement was "mostly an accounting event" whose gap "does not close." The day went on to deliver 19 items / 36 SP. The 2026-09-16 read finds 19 genuine Done transitions worth 37 SP in 24.5 hours, with only 3 SP arriving pre-finished — the inverse of the 2026-09-15 ratio. A 9am Pacific cadence structurally under-reads the current day; the day-over-day delta, not the same-day figure, is the trustworthy number.

The biggest remaining risk is that 2026-09-15 was a catch-up day, not a new rate. S6 needs 26.8 SP per working day across 6 remaining working days against a sprint-to-date 12.5. The projection range is 65–91% of SP, versus S5's 90.7% and 6801's 91%. If the next two reads show 8–12 SP/day rather than 20+, the sprint lands near 65% and roughly 80 SP will need somewhere to go — better decided on Day 9 than Day 14.

Three positives, and they are structural rather than cosmetic. The zero-delivery cohort collapsed from 12 people / 110 SP to 5 people / 48 SP. Not-started SP fell 39.8% → 26.4% for a third consecutive day. And the review queue drained (8 → 6 items), so the back-half review bottleneck flagged on 2026-09-15 did not materialise.

Contamination tagging is not a gap this run — 11/11 on true injections, 13/14 overall. But measured on sprint-entry date instead of created, coverage on mid-sprint arrivals is 13/17 = 76.5%, below threshold. Two smaller regressions to watch: Done-items-without-resolution-dates went 1 → 2, and TRI-56287 was renamed and closed in the same window, meaning a 2-SP performance investigation was silently replaced by a different deliverable. Neither is urgent; both are the kind of thing that only shows up in a roster diff.
