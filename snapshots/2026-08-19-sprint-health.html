Assessment

Completion pace is still the biggest risk, but it moved in the right direction today. At Day 8 of 15 the sprint is 31.8% done by item and 26.8% done by story point against a 53.3% linear ideal — 21.5 and 26.5 points behind respectively, both narrowed from 25.2 and 29.1 on 2026-08-18 on the back of the strongest single day of the sprint (11 items / 17 SP closed from existing scope). The SP gap remains wider than the item gap, so the larger stories are still the laggards.

The arithmetic on the remaining seven days is the real concern. 144.5 SP remain, requiring about 20.6 SP/day to close clean. The prior sprint averaged roughly 12.2 SP/day across its full run; this sprint has averaged 6.6 SP/day over eight days. Those two rates project a close between 50% and 70% completion — roughly 59 to 98 SP of carryover against the baseline sprint's 20 SP (9%). The honest read is that this sprint will carry over materially more than the baseline unless scope is deliberately cut in the next two or three days, and the five quietly-dropped pre-sprint items suggest cutting has already started informally.

Volatility crossed a threshold without changing verdict. Item-level injection is at 10.9%, past the ~10% guideline for the first time; SP-level is 9.1% and still inside it. Absolute injection remains under half the baseline's final rate, so "moderately volatile" still fits — but 83% of all injection SP arrived in the 2026-08-17/2026-08-18 window, and at the current run-rate the sprint reaches baseline volatility by close. The composition is more informative than the total: Dependency Unblock (×3) is now the most common reason, ahead of Mis-Planning (×2) and Stakeholder Urgent (×2), pointing at blocked-work handoffs rather than stakeholder interruption as the dominant driver.

Two positives. Estimation coverage holds at 100% with nothing above 3 SP, so sizing discipline is intact eight days in. And tagging on true injections is still above the 80% threshold at 83.3%, with the entire remaining gap belonging to one assignee — a solvable conversation rather than a process rebuild.
