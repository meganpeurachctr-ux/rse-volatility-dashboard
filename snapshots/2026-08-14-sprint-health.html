The Scope Growth the Framework Cannot See

Between the 2026-08-13 10:53 PDT reading and the 2026-08-14 10:48 PDT reading, scope went from 98 items / 179.5 SP to 102 items / 184.5 SP. Only one of those four items — TRI-55379 — was created after sprint start. The other three items and 4 SP were created before 2026-08-12 13:44 PDT and were added to the sprint board afterward.

The creation-date framework classifies all three as "pre-sprint," which is correct by its own definition and misleading in effect. Work pulled from the backlog into a running sprint consumes exactly as much capacity as work invented mid-sprint; the only difference is when someone typed it. The framework reports 1.0% item injection while the board actually absorbed 3.9% more items in 24 hours.

Identifying the three specific issues requires per-issue changelog reads, which the current Jira MCP tooling cannot do in bulk — sprint-membership change is not exposed in the issue fields and the fields parameter is ignored, so a 102-issue diff is not affordable in one run. The durable fix is to persist the full issue-key list in each snapshot so the next run can diff key sets directly instead of inferring from totals. One-line change to the snapshot writer; it should land before the next sprint.

This is the third instance of the same class of problem in this series, after the close-day in-place rescopes and the late re-estimations found in RSE 26Q3 S3. Scope changes that do not create an issue are invisible to every metric currently computed.

Assessment

The sprint is genuinely healthy on the volatility measures this series exists to track: one true mid-sprint injection at 1 SP, fully tagged, closed in 13 minutes, against a baseline sprint that finished at 21% item injection. The day-1 dump closed small at 4 items, and estimation coverage is still 100% with nothing sized above 3 SP. Completion of 2.9% against a 20.0% ideal looks bad and should not be read as bad — RSE 26Q3 S3 recorded roughly a third of its scope complete on close day, and until that status-lag question is answered the mid-sprint burndown on this board carries no predictive weight. The 26→34 rise in In Progress is the more trustworthy signal, and it points the right way.

The biggest risk remains visibility, not velocity, and it has now gone two days without a fix. TRI-54246 and TRI-54915 are both actively being worked and both attached only to a closed sprint, which means one engineer's entire queue does not appear in any sprint artifact. This is a manual board correction someone needs to make.

The finding to carry into retro is the scope growth: +4 items and +5 SP in 24 hours, of which the injection framework can only account for one. Three items were pulled from backlog into a running sprint, which costs capacity exactly like an injection does but registers as "pre-sprint" because of when the ticket was typed. The framework measures ticket creation, not scope commitment, and those are not the same thing. The fix is cheap — persist the issue-key list in each snapshot — and it should land before the next sprint opens.

Contamination tagging is not a real gap this run. The 40% headline is an artifact of counting day-1 dump items the process deliberately does not nudge; on the population the process actually targets, coverage is 100%.
