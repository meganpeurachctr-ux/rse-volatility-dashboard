Assessment

The biggest risk is that the 2026-09-15 headline improvement is mostly an accounting event. Completion moved from 8.0% to 18.0% of SP, but 10 of those 25 new SP walked in already finished and 4 of the 11 arrivals predate the sprint. Stripping the import out, S6 stands at 14.8% of SP at Day 7 of 15 against a 46.7% ideal. Separately, 2 items / 4 SP of the 42 SP booked were resolved before the sprint even opened (TRI-55708, TRI-56082).

The second risk is concentration. John Shepherd is 50% of everything delivered and 10 of the 11 items added on 2026-09-15. Twelve of twenty-two assignees have delivered nothing — 110 SP, 47.1% of the sprint, belongs to people who have not closed an item. That is a bus-factor problem and a signal that nominal scope was never distributed in a way anyone planned to execute.

A third, quieter risk: In Review grew 1 → 8 between the two reads. Review, not development, may become the constraint in the back half.

The measurement gap to fix: the created-date framework declared this sprint “Healthy” on 2026-09-14 and would have again on 2026-09-15, because it cannot see scope that arrives as pre-written tickets. Only the day-over-day roster diff caught the 11-item import. If injection is going to be governed, sprint-entry date — not created — has to be the trigger for both the metric and the nudge.
